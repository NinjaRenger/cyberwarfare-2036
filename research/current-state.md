# Current State — 2026

## Purpose

This document establishes the technological and cybersecurity baseline from which the Cyberwarfare 2036 scenarios will be developed.

The purpose is to document capabilities and trends that exist today before projecting how they may evolve over the next decade.

## Key Areas of Observation

### Artificial Intelligence

- AI assistants and coding systems
- AI agents capable of performing multi-step tasks
- AI-assisted security analysis
- Automated reasoning and planning
- AI model deployment in enterprise environments

### Offensive Cybersecurity

- AI-assisted reconnaissance
- Automated vulnerability discovery
- AI-assisted exploit research
- Social engineering and phishing automation
- Malware analysis and generation
- Automated attack workflows

### Defensive Cybersecurity

- AI-assisted Security Operations Centers
- Automated detection and response
- AI-assisted threat hunting
- Behavioral analysis
- Automated vulnerability management

### Infrastructure

- Cloud computing
- Identity and access management
- Software supply chains
- Critical infrastructure
- Internet-connected devices
- AI infrastructure and model supply chains

## Key Question

What can AI systems demonstrably do in cybersecurity in 2026?

This question will establish the baseline for future projections.

## Evidence Standard

Major claims should be supported by reliable sources, such as:

- Academic research
- Government publications
- Cybersecurity organizations
- Security research teams
- Technical documentation
- Reputable industry research
